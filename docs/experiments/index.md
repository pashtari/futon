# Experiments

The paper's experiments are one protocol applied to four tasks, and this section documents them in enough detail to rerun them.

- [Benchmarks](benchmarks.md): the protocol, and every model's size, training time and final metrics on Kodak images, Stanford occupancy volumes, Blender radiance fields and DIV2K super-resolution, with convergence plots and qualitative comparisons.
- [Ablations](ablations.md): FUTON's basis, the number of components against the rank, the combiner and the decoder on images, and the number of components against the decoder's width on every task.
- [Reproducing the paper](reproduce.md): the data, the training scripts and their configs, the run directories, and the report script that turns runs into figures and tables.

## The protocol in one paragraph

Every model of a task is given the same parameter budget (about 197k for images, 131k for volumes, 74k for a radiance field, its colour network included, and 500k for super-resolution), the hyperparameters of its authors' code wherever the budget allows them (FUTON's components per axis and decoder width are chosen per task from its [components-width study](ablations.md#components-against-width-on-every-task): half the resolution and 64 units on images, volumes and radiance fields, where FUTON reads its basis from a table of 2048 points per axis, and the low-resolution grid and 128 units for super-resolution; its CP rank is the largest multiple of 5 that keeps it smaller than every baseline), and a learning rate from the grid {3e-1, 1e-1, 3e-2, 1e-2, 3e-3, 1e-3, 3e-4, 1e-4} chosen on two or three signals of the task. Every model trains with PyTorch's default Adam. Images, volumes and super-resolution train for 1000 steps with cosine annealing to a hundredth of the learning rate, on the whole image, on $2^{18}$ random voxels, and on 135 random blocks of 8 × 8 low-resolution pixels, about $2^{18}$ high-resolution ones, through the 4× downsampling operator and with the L1 loss; radiance fields train for 25,000 steps of 4096 rays with exponential decay to a tenth, torch-ngp's recipe. Every run uses seed 0 on an NVIDIA A100, its model compiled with `torch.compile` in a warm-up pass that keeps compilation out of the clock. Costs come from the same runs: the training time counts the training steps alone, every evaluation records how long it took and its peak memory, and every training step its own peak; each cost in the tables is the median over the signals of a task, so a run slowed by other work on its GPU does not move it. Metrics are computed on the full signal (all pixels, all voxels, all 200 test views, the whole high-resolution image), LPIPS with its VGG network, and the tables report the mean over the signals of a task with a within-signal standard error (a plain one for IoU, which saturates on every shape), the best value in bold and the second in italics.
